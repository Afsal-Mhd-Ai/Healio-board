# ORBITAL

**Know your signal.**

ORBITAL is an offline-first astronaut health check-in prototype. It helps a crew member review personal trends, spot readings that may deserve attention, and prepare a concise handoff for a flight surgeon. It is a challenge demo, not a medical device or a substitute for mission procedures.

## Try it

Open `index.html` in a current desktop or mobile browser. There is no install, build step, account, server, or network connection required. The reference links on the Sources screen need internet, but the app itself does not make network requests.

Use **Mission / simulator** to load the clearly labeled 180-day synthetic scenario. Scrub the timeline, play it at different speeds, or inject a sample event to see the dashboard react. Use **Reset scenario** to clear the generated scenario, or **Enter crew data** to switch back to your own entries.

## What’s inside

- A home dashboard with the current mission context, top alerts, next check-in, and an illustrative health score.
- An interactive body-systems map and a visible connection between indicators, spaceflight hazards, and suggested next steps.
- Measurement forms, personal-baseline comparisons, trends, and a guided measurement flow.
- A private wellbeing check-in, countermeasure coach, and offline question-and-answer topics.
- A locally prepared flight-surgeon handoff that can be copied or printed.
- Editable demo thresholds, source notes, and dark/light themes.

## Your data and privacy

Entries are kept in this browser using local storage when available, with an in-memory fallback if storage is unavailable. The app does not upload data. Clearing browser storage removes locally saved entries; the simulator creates synthetic records on this device. Avoid entering sensitive real-world health information into a prototype.

## Important limitations

ORBITAL is not cleared or validated for clinical use, diagnosis, treatment, triage, or fitness-for-duty decisions. Example thresholds, scores, forecasts, and hazard associations are illustrative and configurable; they are not flight rules or personalized medical advice. Follow your mission's approved procedures and contact the appropriate crew or medical support for concerning symptoms.

## Sources

The in-app Sources screen links to NASA's Human Research Program and NASA-STD-3001, as well as public exploration resources from ESA, JAXA, and the Canadian Space Agency. These references provide context; they do not validate this prototype's thresholds or recommendations.
